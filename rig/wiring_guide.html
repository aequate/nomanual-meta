NoManual wiring guide

Everything to connect in your 5-hour gap, in order. Finish each stage's test before starting the next, so a fault can only be in what you just added.

Five rules that protect the parts

  1. Change wires only with the adapter unplugged and USB out.
  2. Never connect the 5V rail to any ESP32 pin, including 5V and VIN. The ESP32 gets power from USB only.
  3. Check the capacitor's direction. The side with the stripe is negative. Backwards, it can burst.
  4. Keep the laser out of eyes. Aim it down into a box or at the table, and never at a person or a shiny surface. You're in a shared space, so keep it off unless you're testing it.
  5. Keep the ESP32 off metal, so nothing shorts its pins from below. Keep one hand near the rocker switch whenever the servos have power.

Before you start

Pack tonight

If you can't get a screwdriver

The DC plug's screw terminals and the bracket both need one, so no servo or laser can get power. You can still do most of the wiring:

  • Do all of stage 1.
  • Do stage 2's parts test, then wire everything except the two wires into the DC plug. Skip the 5 V test.
  • In stage 3, connect the capacitor, the servo wires and the ground link. Skip the test.
  • In stage 5, connect the laser's wires only if you've read its pin labels. Don't mount it.

Then, at home with a screwdriver, connect the DC plug and run the tests from stage 2 onward. Stop and do the 5 V test before anything else gets power.

Foam, cardboard, paper, ruler and glue aren't needed for this session.

Stage 2: power wiring

about 1 hour, adapter unplugged
  1. Test the parts first. Set the meter to the beep symbol. The fuse should beep or read near 0. The switch should beep when ON and show 1 when OFF.
  2. Fit the fuse in its holder.
  3. Positive path, red wire: DC plug + → fuse holder → WAGO A → rocker switch → +5V rail.
  4. Negative path, black wire: DC plug − → WAGO B. Then a short black link from WAGO B to the GND rail.
  5. Tug every wire. Strip about 1 cm for each WAGO, and tape any bare metal on the switch.

WAGO A joins the fuse holder's lead to the switch's lead. If your wires already reach each other, soldered or otherwise, you can skip it.

Test it

  1. Switch OFF. Put the meter on 20 in the DCV section: black probe in COM, red in VΩmA.
  2. Plug in the adapter and turn the switch ON. Measure with red on +5V rail, black on GND rail, using the WAGO test holes or bare wire.

Pass: 4.8 to 5.3 V with the switch ON, and 0 V with it OFF.

A reading near −5 V means the wires are swapped at the DC plug. A reading of 0 V with the switch ON means a loose wire or a blown fuse. Unplug before fixing either.

Stage 3: capacitor and bare servos

about 45 min
  1. Everything unplugged. Fit the capacitor: stripe side to the GND rail, other side to the +5V rail. Recheck 5 V as in stage 2, then unplug again.
  2. Don't fit the servos to the bracket or attach the servo arms yet.
  3. Plug each servo into a servo extension. Push 3 male-to-male jumpers into the extension's far end.
  4. Brown → GND rail. Red → +5V rail. Tug each one, because jumper pins can slip out of a WAGO.
  5. Orange → its own breadboard row, then a male-to-female jumper from that row to the ESP32: pan to GPIO 18, tilt to GPIO 19. Swapping these two later is how Demo C works.
  6. Connect ground: a wire from your breadboard ground to WAGO B. Without this, the servos jitter or ignore commands.
  7. Leave slack in the tilt servo's wires, because that servo rides on top of the pan servo.

Test it

All DIP switches OFF, USB in, adapter in, then rocker switch ON with your hand on it. Each servo may jump once as power comes on, because it snaps to the middle. That's normal with no bracket fitted. Run the console, then type these one at a time:

.venv\Scripts\python scripts\rig_console.py --port COM4
j 0.5 0.5
j 0.6 0.5
j 0.5 0.6
j 0.5 0.5

Pass: the first move changes nothing or barely moves, 0.6 0.5 turns only the pan servo, and 0.5 0.6 turns only the tilt servo, all slowly. If they're swapped, swap the two orange wires. Stay on j 0.5 0.5 for the next stage.

Stage 4: build the bracket

about 45 min, servos centred
  1. With the servos at j 0.5 0.5, turn the rocker switch OFF. Don't turn the shafts by hand from here on.
  2. Follow the bracket's own assembly order. Fit each servo arm so that the bracket sits at the middle of its travel, with the top plate level and facing forward.
  3. Check that each joint can swing freely about 45° each way. If an arm is off centre, take it off and refit it one spline over.
  4. Tighten the screws until snug, without forcing them into the plastic.
  5. Tie-wrap or tape the bracket's base to something heavy, such as a book or a box. The pan motion will otherwise rock it.

Test it

Rocker ON, hand on it. This runs the first-power checks and asks you y/n questions. Close the console first (q), since only one program can use the port.

.venv\Scripts\python scripts\rig_bringup.py calib --port COM4
  • "Did both servos move?" They're already at the middle, so they may not move. Answer y if nothing jerked.
  • "Which way did the tip move?" There's no tip yet, so type how the top plate moved: left, right, up or down. It asks once for each servo. Note both answers.
  • The corners question also mentions the board and camera, which you don't have yet. Answer on buzzing only. We'll redo that part on the real board.

Pass: it prints ALL PASS. If a servo buzzes against a stop, turn the rocker OFF at once. That means an arm needs refitting. It's safe to run again afterwards.

Stage 5: laser

about 20 min, rocker OFF
  1. Check the module's pins. It usually has S, a middle pin and −. Connect S → +5V rail and − → GND rail, leaving the middle pin empty. If it has two bare wires, red goes to + and black to −.
  2. Aim it down into your box before powering it.
  3. Rocker ON. Pass: a red dot appears on the box. If nothing happens, turn it OFF, recheck the pins, and send me a photo of the module's labels. Don't guess at other pin combinations.
  4. Turn the lens gently to get a small, sharp dot about 14 cm away.
  5. Rocker OFF. Tie-wrap the module flat to the top plate, keeping its beam straight and its wires slack.

The rocker powers the laser and the servos together, so a moving bracket would sweep the beam around the room. With the rocker OFF, pull the laser's + wire out of the WAGO before stage 6, and leave it out until you have the real board.

Stage 6: run mode check

about 15 min, laser + wire out
  1. Rocker OFF. DIP switch 1 ON and the rest OFF.
  2. Rocker ON, hand on it, then run the command below. The bracket should glide slowly to its start position, and everything after that should keep it still.
  3. It asks about a "start mark" you don't have yet. Answer y if the bracket moved slowly or stayed put. Answer y to "stay still" if it didn't move during the checks.
.venv\Scripts\python scripts\rig_bringup.py run --port COM4 --draw 1

Pass: it prints ALL PASS.

Then test your cut-off. In the console, run r 0 0 0 0 2 and turn the rocker OFF during those 2 seconds. Then type p: the ESP32 should still answer. Rocker back ON with your hand on it, then set DIP switches back to OFF.
